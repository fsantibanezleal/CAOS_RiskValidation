# 01. Hull-White one-factor

Module `riskvalidation.ccr.hw1f`. Sources: dossier 05 C.4; the U9 research addendum R4 (the closed forms read from QuantLib 1.44,
`ql/models/shortrate/onefactormodels/hullwhite.hpp` and `hullwhite.cpp`, and `ql/pricingengines/swaption/jamshidianswaptionengine.cpp`,
fetched from `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/lballabio/QuantLib/v1.44/`) and R6 (Brigo, Dalessandro, Neugebauer and Triki, "A stochastic
processes toolkit for risk management", arXiv 0812.4210, section "Mean reverting processes: Vasicek"). The curve is passed as two
callables on arrays of years, `p0(t)` for today's discount factor $P(0,t)$ and `forward(t)` for the instantaneous forward $f(0,t)$;
`curve_functions(zero_rates, tenors)` builds them from a zero curve through `irrbb.curves.zero_rates_at` and
`irrbb.simulation._forward_curve` (imported, never copied; `irrbb.simulation.hw1f_scenarios` is unchanged, contract
`engine-irrbb-api`).

## The model and the fit to today's curve

The short rate is $r_t=\varphi(t)+x_t$ with $x_t$ an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process, $dx=-a\,x\,dt+\sigma\,dW$, $x(0)=0$
(QuantLib `HullWhite::Dynamics`), and the fitting parameter is (QuantLib `FittingParameter`)

$$\varphi(t)=f(0,t)+\frac12\left[\frac{\sigma\,(1-e^{-at})}{a}\right]^2$$

`phi(t, a, sigma, forward)`. For $a<\sqrt{\varepsilon}$ the code uses $\sigma t$ in place of the bracket. Invariants: $\varphi(0)=f(0,0)$;
$\sigma=0$ gives $f(0,t)$. Golden: a flat forward 0.03, $a=0.1$, $\sigma=0.01$, $t=2$ gives `0.030164`. The equivalent form of dossier 05 C.4,
$dr=(\theta(t)-a\,r)dt+\sigma\,dW$ with $\theta(t)=\partial_t f(0,t)+a\,f(0,t)+\frac{\sigma^2}{2a}(1-e^{-2at})$, follows by own algebra (the
addendum derives it; the textbook citation in the dossier, Brigo and Mercurio, remains UNVERIFIED as to edition and page).

## The affine bond price

$$P(t,T)=A(t,T)\,e^{-B(t,T)\,r_t},\quad B(t,T)=\frac{1-e^{-a(T-t)}}{a},\quad A(t,T)=\frac{P(0,T)}{P(0,t)}\exp\!\Big[B\,f(0,t)-\frac{\sigma^2}{4a}\big(1-e^{-2at}\big)B^2\Big]$$

`bond_b`, `bond_a`, `bond_price` (QuantLib `HullWhite::A`; the code writes the last term as $0.25(\sigma B)^2B(0,2t)$, which the engine
does too, so the limit $a\to0$ needs no division). $B(t,t)=0$ and $A(t,t)=1$; for $a<\sqrt{\varepsilon}$, $B=T-t$.

Two identities worth knowing. First, at $t=0$ the factor is $A(0,T)=P(0,T)\,e^{B(0,T)f(0,0)}$, not $P(0,T)$: the golden for a flat 3 percent
curve, $a=0.1$, $T=5$ is $A=e^{-0.15}e^{3.934693\times0.03}=0.968546$ against $P(0,5)=0.860708$. Second, the repricing identity is
`bond_price` at $t=0$ with $r=\varphi(0)=f(0,0)$, where the factor $e^{-B\,r}$ cancels it: the price equals $P(0,T)$ to 1e-12 for every $T$ of the
curve and any $a$ and $\sigma$ (the test checks six tenors and six more maturities on two curves). $B(0,5)$ with $a=0.1$ is `3.934693`.

## Simulation

`simulate_state(grid, a, sigma, n_paths, seed, antithetic=True)` returns (P, K) paths of $x$ with $x(0)=0$ on a grid that starts at 0 and strictly
increases, by the exact discretisation (addendum R6, equations 49 to 52)

$$x(t_i)=x(t_{i-1})\,e^{-a\Delta t}+\sigma\sqrt{\frac{1-e^{-2a\Delta t}}{2a}}\,z_i,\qquad z_i\sim N(0,1)$$

so the law does not depend on the step. Antithetic: path $i+P/2$ is minus path $i$, so the sample mean is zero to 1e-12 and `n_paths` must be
even. $\mathrm{Var}\,x(t)=\sigma^2(1-e^{-2at})/(2a)$; golden at $a=0.1$, $\sigma=0.01$, $t=5$: `0.00031606`, and the sample variance of 20,000
antithetic paths lies within three standard errors on a one-step and on a 60-step grid.

`simulate_short_rate(grid, a, sigma, forward, n_paths, seed)` adds $\varphi(t)$. Two checks: the mean of $r(t)$ is $\varphi(t)$, and the
discounted bond prices are martingales, $E[e^{-\int_0^t r}\,P(t,T;r_t)]=P(0,T)$ at every grid point (test `ccr.martingale_discounted_bonds`).
`bank_account(grid, short_rate, a, sigma, p0, forward)` is the numeraire $N(t)=e^{\int_0^t r}$ of the paths. The deterministic part is integrated exactly,
$\int_0^t\varphi=-\ln P(0,t)+\frac{\sigma^2}{2a^2}\left[t-\frac{2(1-e^{-at})}{a}+\frac{1-e^{-2at}}{2a}ight]$ ($\sigma^2t^3/6$ as $a	o0$; a power series in $at$ below $at=0.5$, so the closed form never loses digits to cancellation), and only the state $x=r-\varphi$ is integrated by the trapezoid rule on the grid. A trapezoid on $r$ itself misses $\int\varphi$ by a grid-dependent amount (the forward of a zero curve jumps at the pillars: 0.2 percent at five years on a semiannual grid), a bias in every discounted exposure and CVA. At $\sigma=0$, $1/N(t)=P(0,t)$ to 1e-12 on any grid. It is the numeraire for `exposure.expected_exposure` in the discounted measure.

## The zero-coupon bond option

For an option expiring at $T$ on a zero-coupon bond maturing at $S$ with strike $K$ (a bond price), QuantLib `HullWhite::discountBondOption`:

$$\sigma_p=\sigma\,B(T,S)\sqrt{\frac{1-e^{-2aT}}{2a}},\qquad \mathrm{ZBC}=P(0,S)N(h)-K\,P(0,T)N(h-\sigma_p),\quad h=\frac{\ln\frac{P(0,S)}{K\,P(0,T)}}{\sigma_p}+\frac{\sigma_p}{2}$$

`bond_option_vol` returns $\sigma_p$ (for $a<\sqrt{\varepsilon}$, $\sigma\,B\sqrt T$) and `bond_option` the price. Golden: $a=0.1$, $\sigma=0.01$, $T=2$,
$S=5$ gives $\sigma_p=0.033276$ (tolerance 5e-7). Put-call parity $\mathrm{ZBC}-\mathrm{ZBP}=P(0,S)-K\,P(0,T)$ holds to 1e-12; the price is non-negative
and agrees with the Monte Carlo of `bond_price` under the bank account numeraire within the error (100,000 paths). With $\sigma_p=0$ the price
is the forward intrinsic value. The standard form of the call (the "blackFormula with unit discount") is the addendum's derived reading of the
QuantLib call.

## The Jamshidian swaption

`swaption_jamshidian(is_payer, expiry, fixed_times, fixed_amounts, a, sigma, p0, forward, nominal=None)` prices the European swaption on the
remaining swap starting at the expiry $T_e$ (QuantLib `jamshidianswaptionengine.cpp`, addendum R4). With fixed payment times $T_i$, coupon
amounts $c_i$ and the nominal $N$ added to the last:

1. find $r^*$ with $N-\sum_i c_i\,P(T_e,T_i;r^*)=0$ by Brent on $[-10,10]$;
2. the leg strikes are $K_i=P(T_e,T_i;r^*)$;
3. the value is $\sum_i c_i\,\mathrm{ZBO}(K_i)$, a put for a payer swaption and a call for a receiver.

Payer minus receiver equals the forward swap value, $N\,P(0,T_e)-\sum_i c_i P(0,T_i)$, to 1e-9; the value is non-negative, tends to the intrinsic
value as $\sigma\to0$ and is zero for a payer swaption far out of the money at $\sigma=0$. The test checks the parity on five fixed rates and the
Monte Carlo of the payoff at the expiry for both types within four standard errors (200,000 paths). QuantLib's tolerance for $r^*$ is 1e-8; the
engine solves to 1e-12 so that the parity holds to 1e-9. The contract's signature has no nominal; `nominal` is an optional trailing argument,
inferred as the last amount minus the first when the coupons are constant and otherwise required (a one-payment leg cannot be split into coupon
and nominal). It is the one extension of the signature and is reported for the contract's next revision.

## The AR(1) calibration

`calibrate_ar1(dates, short_rate, before, min_years=3.0, dt=None)` returns an `Ar1Fit` (`a`, `sigma`, `theta`, `a_se`, `sigma_se`, `n_obs`,
`sample_start`, `sample_end`). With the exact discretisation $x(t_i)=c+b\,x(t_{i-1})+\delta\,\epsilon$, OLS of $x(t_i)$ on $x(t_{i-1})$ gives $c$, $b$,
$\delta$ and (addendum R6, equations 55 to 57, Brigo et al.)

$$a=-\frac{\ln b}{\Delta t},\qquad \theta=\frac{c}{1-b},\qquad \sigma=\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{\dfrac{(b^2-1)\,\Delta t}{2\ln b}}}$$

`ar1_to_hw1f(b, delta, dt)` is the map. Golden: $b=0.99$, $\delta=0.002$, $\Delta t=1/12$ give $a=0.120604$ and $\sigma=0.006963$. Only observations
strictly before `before` are used (the result's `sample_end` is before it: the "no look-ahead" check); fewer than `min_years` of history
raises (CRE53.51: at least three years); $b\notin(0,1)$ raises (the series is not mean-reverting). The residual spread is
$\delta=\sqrt{SSE/(m-2)}$ with $m$ pairs. The standard errors drive the sensitivity of EE and PFE to the short-rate calibration: $a_{se}=se(b)/(b\,\Delta t)$
by the delta method, and $\sigma_{se}$ by the delta method on $(b,\delta)$ with the OLS standard error of $b$ and the normal-theory
$\delta/\sqrt{2(m-2)}$ of the residual spread, taken as independent. The default step is the median spacing of the dates in years of 365.25 days.
A series simulated from `simulate_state` with known $a$ and $\sigma$ is recovered within three standard errors (1,200 monthly observations; all 8
seeds of the test are within, the largest at 2.5 standard errors, and the test requires at least 7).

Caveat (addendum R6): the AR(1) fit gives the physical-measure $a$, $\theta$ and $\sigma$ of one short-rate proxy; the Hull-White fit to today's
curve needs the risk-neutral drift $\varphi(t)$, so the historical fit supplies $a$ and $\sigma$ only.

## Limits

- The journal derivations (Hull and White 1990, RFS 3(4):573-592; Hull and White 1994, Journal of Derivatives; Jamshidian 1989, Journal of
  Finance 44(1):205-209) were not read as papers: UNVERIFIED. The closed forms are transcribed from QuantLib 1.44, and the C12 bake reconciles
  the engine numerically against it.
- One factor, a constant $a$ and a constant $\sigma$; the swaption is European, with zero spread and a constant nominal (as the QuantLib engine
  requires).
- The numeraire integral is a trapezoid on the grid, so the martingale check holds to the Monte Carlo error and not to rounding.
